FROM PUBLIC DATA TO RESEARCH

A guided introductory session for exploring public data, thinking critically with AI, formulating useful questions, and interpreting basic analytical information.

Welcome / Training Overview

Who is this session for?

This workshop is designed for participants from diverse academic or professional backgrounds, including those with limited prior experience in mathematics, statistics, data analysis, or AI.

The session introduces a guided workflow and practical examples. It does not assume advanced technical knowledge and does not aim to create expert-level competency within two hours.

Session Principle

AI should support thinking, not replace it.

Use AI to explore, question, clarify and evaluate ideas. Verify important academic information, inspect evidence, and keep final academic judgement with the user.

Training Objectives

By the end of this introductory session, participants should be able to:

1

Describe the purpose of the training workflow in simple terms and recognise how the main stages connect.

2

Identify and explore credible public-data resources relevant to a simple topic or discipline-specific question.

3

Evaluate AI-generated information using basic criteria such as accuracy, evidence, credibility, currency, bias and limitations.

4

Formulate more purposeful questions and prompts that encourage explanation, comparison, evidence checking and reflection.

5

Apply a guided process to a simple analytical example and interpret basic regression information at an introductory level.

6

Use selected AI-assisted prompts with guidance while recognising that AI suggestions require verification and human judgement.

7

Reflect on one or more ideas, resources or approaches that could be adapted to their own teaching, learning, research or professional context.

Stage 01

DISCOVER

What can I find out?

Discover is the exploration stage. The goal is to widen your understanding before deciding what is important, correct, or useful.

✦Explore first. Conclude later.

Use credible public repositories to locate datasets, documentation and contextual information before choosing a research direction.

B. Discover Ideas with AI

Explore unfamiliar topics, terminology, perspectives, resources, approaches, possible gaps and questions worth investigating.

🔎 Unfamiliar topics
🏷️ Important keywords
🔗 Related concepts
⚖️ Different perspectives
🧭 Possible approaches
❓ Questions worth investigating

How to Use DISCOVER

1. Start Broad

Ask for an overview before requesting conclusions.

2. Identify Concepts

Find terminology, theories, variables, methods and debates.

3. Explore Perspectives

Ask how different disciplines or researchers may view the topic.

4. Generate Directions

Brainstorm possibilities without treating them as final decisions.

5. Identify Gaps

Ask what may be missing or what information is still needed.

Example

Topic: Student engagement in online learning

Basic

“What are the main factors commonly discussed in relation to student engagement in online learning?”

Follow-up

“Group these factors into student-related, teacher-related, technology-related, and course-design factors.”

Then

“Which of these factors could reasonably be investigated using survey data?”

Purpose: not to decide which factor is correct, but to discover the possibilities.

Try Asking AI

Prompt 1 — Explore a Topic

I am new to [TOPIC]. Give me a structured overview of the main concepts, important terminology, and related ideas I should understand before studying it in detail.

Prompt 2 — Generate Keywords

Generate 15 useful academic search keywords and keyword combinations related to [TOPIC]. Group them into broad, specific, and methodological terms.

Prompt 3 — Explore Perspectives

Show me several different perspectives or approaches to understanding [TOPIC]. Explain briefly how each perspective differs.

Prompt 4 — Identify Gaps

Based on this topic, what important questions or areas might someone overlook? Do not assume these are proven research gaps; present them as possible areas for further investigation.

Prompt 5 — Map the Topic

Create a beginner-friendly concept map in text form for [TOPIC], showing the main concept, related concepts, possible variables, and possible applications.

💡 KEY TIP

Do not stop at the first AI response. Ask follow-up questions, request alternative perspectives, and compare different explanations. AI can help expand the search space, but you remain responsible for deciding what is relevant and worth investigating.

Stage 02

EVALUATE

Can I trust or use this information?

Evaluate means critically examining information before deciding whether it is reliable, relevant, and appropriate to use.

Important distinction

AI-GENERATED SUGGESTION ≠ VERIFIED ACADEMIC EVIDENCE

AI-generated information should not automatically be treated as verified fact.

Accuracy

Is the information factually correct?

Relevance

Does it actually answer the question?

Credibility

Is it supported by trustworthy expertise or reliable sources?

Evidence

What evidence supports the statement?

Currency

Is the information current enough?

Bias

Is one perspective presented as if it were the only valid view?

Consistency

Does it agree with other credible sources?

Limitations / Source Quality

What uncertainty, weakness or source problem is missing?

Example

AI claim:

“Online learning always increases student independence.”

→ What evidence supports this claim?

→ Is the word “always” justified?

→ What conditions might produce different outcomes?

Important claims should then be checked against suitable academic or authoritative sources.

Before accepting an AI response, ask:

Try Asking AI

Prompt 1 — Critical Review

Review the following response critically. Separate factual claims, interpretations, assumptions, and recommendations. Identify which claims require external verification.

Prompt 2 — Find Weaknesses

Identify possible weaknesses, unsupported assumptions, missing context, or overgeneralisation in the following explanation: [PASTE TEXT].

Prompt 3 — Compare Explanations

Compare these two explanations of [TOPIC]. Identify where they agree, where they differ, and what evidence would be needed to determine which interpretation is better supported.

Prompt 4 — Evidence Check

For each major claim in the following text, tell me what type of evidence or source would be appropriate for verification. Do not invent references.

Prompt 5 — Bias and Alternatives

Examine the following response for possible bias or one-sided framing. Suggest alternative interpretations or perspectives that should also be considered.

⚠️ KEY TIP

Fluent language is not evidence of accuracy. A response can sound professional and still be wrong. Treat important academic claims as something to verify, not simply something to believe.

Stage 03

UNDERSTAND

Do I really understand it?

Understand means moving beyond receiving an answer to understanding the meaning, reasoning, relationships, and implications behind it.

ANSWER→MEANING→APPLICATION
🧠 SIMPLIFY

Translate technical ideas into clearer language.

🔄 RE-EXPLAIN

Explain the same idea using another approach.

⚖️ COMPARE

Show similarities and differences.

📚 GIVE EXAMPLES

Connect abstract ideas to realistic situations.

🔗 CONNECT IDEAS

Relate new knowledge to prior knowledge.

⚠️ MISCONCEPTIONS

Identify common misunderstandings.

💡 ANALOGIES

Use intuitive comparisons while noting their limits.

🪜 BREAK DOWN

Present complex processes as manageable steps.

Example

Concept: Correlation does not imply causation

Basic: “Define correlation.”
Better: “Explain why correlation does not prove causation using a simple example.”
Deeper: “What alternative explanations could produce a correlation between two variables?”
Further: “Give me a situation where causal interpretation might be more defensible.”

From Answer → To Understanding

Answer-focused

“What is a confidence interval?”

Understanding-focused

“Explain what a confidence interval means in practical terms and identify one common misconception.”

Deeper understanding

“Give me two examples showing correct and incorrect interpretations of a 95% confidence interval.”

The first question obtains information. The later questions encourage understanding.

Try Asking AI

Prompt 1 — Beginner Explanation

Explain [CONCEPT] to a beginner using simple language, but keep the important academic meaning.

Prompt 2 — Explain With an Example

Explain [CONCEPT] using one simple real-world example. Then explain exactly how the example represents the concept.

Prompt 3 — Compare Concepts

Compare [CONCEPT A] and [CONCEPT B]. Explain their similarities, differences, and when each is used.

Prompt 4 — Find Misconceptions

What common misconceptions might a beginner have about [CONCEPT]? Explain why each misconception is incorrect.

Prompt 5 — Explain Another Way

I still do not understand this explanation: [PASTE TEXT]. Explain it in a different way using simpler language, an analogy, and a short example.

🧠 KEY TIP

If you can only repeat the AI's answer, you may not yet understand it. Ask: Why? How? Can you give me an example? What would be a counterexample? How is this different from something similar? What mistake might I make here?

Stage 04

QUESTION

What should I ask next?

Better questioning improves thinking, not just AI output. It helps clarify ideas, expose assumptions, identify evidence, recognise uncertainty, compare alternatives and move from description to analysis.

A useful questioning progression

RECALL

What is it?

EXPLAIN

Why does it happen?

APPLY

How would I use it?

ANALYSE

How are the parts related?

EVALUATE

How strong is the evidence?

CREATE

What could I propose or investigate?

This is a useful progression, not a rigid rule. Choose the level that fits your learning purpose.

Clarification

“What exactly does this term mean?”

Probing

“Why does this conclusion follow?”

Comparison

“How is Method A different from Method B?”

Why

“Why is this assumption important?”

What If

“What might happen if this condition is violated?”

Assumption Checking

“What assumptions does this argument depend on?”

Evidence Seeking

“What evidence would support this claim?”

Reflective

“What have I understood well, and what remains unclear?”

Weak → Better → Deeper

Regression

Weak: “What is regression?”

Better: “Explain the purpose of regression and give a simple real-world example.”

Deeper: “What assumptions should be considered before interpreting a regression model, and what problems could arise if they are violated?”

AI Bias

Weak: “What is AI bias?”

Better: “Explain how bias may appear in AI-generated responses and give two examples.”

Deeper: “How could a user distinguish between bias introduced by the training data, the prompt, and the interpretation of the AI response?”

Source Evaluation

Weak: “Is this source good?”

Better: “Evaluate the credibility of this source using authority, evidence, currency, relevance, and possible bias.”

Deeper: “What additional sources would you consult before relying on this source for an academic conclusion?”

The beginner workflow

1

Regression in Plain English

Regression describes how an outcome variable changes in relation to one or more predictor variables. In simple linear regression, one predictor \(X\) is related to one numerical outcome \(Y\).

Example question

Can study hours help explain or predict students' test scores?

Y: Test Score
X: Study Hours
🧠 Remember

Association or prediction does not automatically establish causation.

2

When Should I Use Regression?

Simple Linear Regression

One numerical outcome \(Y\) and one predictor \(X\).

Multiple Linear Regression

One numerical outcome \(Y\) and two or more predictors.

⚠️ Regression may not be suitable when...

the outcome is not numerical, the relationship is clearly non-linear, data quality is poor, or the study design does not support the intended interpretation.

Good to know: other regression types

The source notes also cover logistic regression for categorical outcomes and multivariate regression for multiple outcomes. They are useful next steps, but the core training here focuses on basic linear regression.

3

Meet the Regression Equation

\[\hat{y}=b_0+b_1x\]
\(\hat y\)
predicted outcome
\(b_0\)
intercept
\(b_1\)
slope / regression coefficient
\(x\)
predictor value

Hypothetical training example

\(\hat y=45+3.2x\)

Each additional study hour is associated with an estimated 3.2-point increase in predicted test score.

Predicted score: 61.0

4

Look at Your Data First

✓ Identify Y and X.

✓ Inspect missing values and obvious errors.

✓ Review descriptive statistics.

✓ Create a scatter plot.

✓ Look for unusual observations / outliers.

✓ Ask whether an approximately linear pattern is plausible.

🛑 STOP & CHECK

Software can print an output even when the model is inappropriate.

5

Run the Regression

  1. 1. Select outcome Y.
  2. 2. Select predictor(s) X.
  3. 3. Fit the model.
  4. 4. Examine coefficient, \(R^2\), p-value and/or confidence interval.
  5. 5. Check diagnostics.
  6. 6. Interpret and report.

The same conceptual workflow applies in R, Python, SPSS, Jamovi, Excel and other statistical software.

6

The Regression Output: Focus Here First

1. Coefficient

Direction and estimated size of change in Y.

2. \(R^2\)

Variation in Y accounted for by the model.

3. p-value / CI

Evidence about whether a coefficient differs from zero.

4. Direction

Positive = upward; negative = downward.

5. Diagnostics

Look for residual patterns and unusual points.

⚠️ Safeguard

p < 0.05 does not prove practical importance, good model fit or causation. High \(R^2\) does not automatically mean a model is valid.

7

How to Interpret Results

Coefficient

“For every one-unit increase in X, predicted Y increases/decreases by approximately ___ units.”

\(R^2\)

“Approximately ___% of observed variation in Y is accounted for by the predictors in this model.”

p-value

A small p-value is evidence against a zero coefficient under the model assumptions; it is not a measure of practical importance.

Prediction

Prefer interpolation within the observed range; extrapolation outside it is risky.

8

🚦 Regression Health Check

✓ Linearity

A straight-line pattern should be a reasonable approximation.

✓ Independence

Problematic dependence should not be present where independence is assumed.

✓ Constant residual variance

Residual spread should be reasonably similar across fitted values.

✓ Residual behaviour

Residual and Q–Q plots can reveal warning signs.

✓ Influential observations

Extreme points can strongly affect the fitted model.

✓ Multicollinearity

In multiple regression, highly correlated predictors can make coefficient interpretation less reliable.

9

Multiple Regression Without the Fear

\[\hat y=b_0+b_1x_1+b_2x_2+\cdots+b_kx_k\]

One numerical outcome, several predictors.

House-price example
  • Y = House price
  • \(X_1\) = House size
  • \(X_2\) = Number of bedrooms
  • \(X_3\) = Distance from city centre

“Holding the other predictors constant” means interpreting one predictor while comparing cases that are similar on the other predictors in the model.

Advanced / Next Step: model selection

The source notes introduce adjusted \(R^2\), Mallows' \(C_p\), AIC, all-possible regression, best-subset regression and stepwise regression. Keep these as advanced tools rather than the beginner starting point.

10

From Output to a Research Statement

❌ Poor

“X causes Y because p < 0.05.”

✅ Better

“X was statistically associated with Y in the fitted model. This result should not automatically be interpreted as causal.”

Reusable reporting template

“A linear regression was used to examine whether [X] was associated with / helped predict [Y]. The estimated coefficient for [X] was [b = ___], indicating that [...]. The model accounted for approximately [\(R^2\times100\)]% of the observed variation in [Y]. [Include uncertainty/significance information where appropriate]. These results indicate an association within the analysed data and should not automatically be interpreted as causal.”

✓ Quick Knowledge Checks

🤖 AI-assisted regression

Use AI as Your Regression Assistant — Not Your Statistician

Use AI to clarify thinking, but verify the model, assumptions and interpretation.

Identify Y and X

I am studying [topic]. My dataset contains these variables: [variables]. My research question is [question]. Help me identify a reasonable dependent variable Y and predictor variable(s) X. Explain your reasoning and flag any ambiguity. Do not invent variables that are not in my dataset.

Regression suitability

Based on the following variables and research question, assess whether linear regression is potentially appropriate. Explain what I should check before running the model. Do not assume that the regression assumptions are satisfied.

Interpret output

Here is my regression output: [paste output]. Explain the coefficient, R², p-value/confidence interval and key diagnostics in beginner-friendly language. Separate statistical interpretation from causal interpretation and do not overclaim.

Check my interpretation

Here is my regression result and my written interpretation: [paste]. Check whether my interpretation is statistically defensible. Identify any overclaiming, especially causal claims, and rewrite it more cautiously if needed.

Explain diagnostics

Here are my regression diagnostic results/plots: [paste or upload]. Explain what each diagnostic is checking, what warning signs are present, and what I should investigate next. Do not claim assumptions are satisfied unless the evidence supports that conclusion.
